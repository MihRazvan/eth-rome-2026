# Private qualification: product and privacy decision

12 September 2026 · Research-only assessment against interface at `fbb76f2d2f1f6a92ed37007a63b26fc4d15e3f28`. No customer contacted, production credential issued, framework installed, or proof benchmark rerun by this worker. Implementation evidence belongs to the parallel proving, transport and contract probes.

**Conditional GO for a bounded technical product demonstration; NO-GO for a general credential marketplace or anonymous-employment claim.** The strongest initial hypothesis is a **portable eligibility gate for confidential, paid technical reviews**, used by a small assessment collective and multiple independent commissioning clients. Its value would be minimizing the qualification information each client receives while preserving current eligibility and an enforceable payment agreement. The unmet demand and willingness to change issuing workflows are not yet established.

The honest claim is: **“Prove you are currently approved for this review. Keep your certificate number to yourself.”** Not: “Nobody can identify you,” “the issuer cannot track you,” “one human, one application,” or “qualification proves the work is good.”

## 1. Initial customer, job and issuer path

The initial customer should be an **existing small security-review collective that vets contributors and commissions bounded second opinions for several project teams**. A task is concrete: assess one allowance flow, review one upgrade diff, or reproduce one reported issue. The collective already knows the reviewer and applies its own admission criteria. A client needs to know that the collective currently permits that reviewer to accept the task class; it need not receive the reviewer's credential serial, full training history or reusable account identifier merely to perform that check.

This is adjacent to a demonstrated paid-work process. Code4rena documents a Certified Security Researcher role requiring performance criteria, accepted tax information, contributor approval and staff review. That supports separating admission/identity handling from the limited credential needed by a prospective client. It does **not** establish that Code4rena exposes this credential externally, supports this circuit, wants anonymous auditors, or would partner. ([Code4rena SR criteria](https://docs.code4rena.com/roles/sr-wardens), accessed 12 September 2026)

The minimum useful workflow is **one trusted issuer, one holder, two genuinely separate client applications, one qualification class and two funded assignments**. The second client matters: a single centrally operated marketplace can already hide its internal user IDs with ordinary database permissions. The proposed product earns its complexity only if clients independently verify one issuance without asking the issuer which credential was presented or depending on the original platform's database.

There are three credible issuer routes:

1. **Prototype:** the team operates a clearly named test assessment and issues test credentials after a documented assessment. This proves the mechanism, not a recognized professional qualification. Do not display another organization's logo or describe a seeded credential as its certification.
2. **First real pilot:** a small collective voluntarily issues *its own* current eligibility attestations using locally generated holder commitments. It defines admission, expiry, revocation, replacement and appeals. Two clients explicitly agree to trust that issuer and class. This does not require importing anyone's university degree or public certification.
3. **Existing credential provider:** integrate its issuance protocol, or obtain explicit reissuance into the supported format. A PDF upload or a public certificate lookup cannot silently become an issuer signature over our holder secret. A bridging attestor would be a new trusted issuer and must be named.

No such pilot partner has been secured. The provider is a prospect category, not a customer. Reviewer reputation can also be commercially important: some clients actively want a named track record. For those clients, hiding only a serial number may not justify new infrastructure. The first discovery question is whether unnecessary credential reuse creates an actual cost or concern, rather than whether people generally like privacy.

An alternative is a **paid accessibility testing panel**. Fable describes assessed, trained and compensated testers and a managed recruiting workflow. Its privacy policy explicitly includes identity, assistive technology, voice/image and test-request data shared with customers when participants choose to perform a request. These are concrete disclosure boundaries, not allegations of misuse. ([Fable testers](https://makeitfable.com/testers/), [privacy policy, 27 June 2025](https://makeitfable.com/privacy-policy/)) A narrow panel-training credential could avoid unnecessary certificate/identity disclosure during initial application. However, recordings, the chosen assistive-technology class and tiny cohorts can identify a person or reveal disability-related information. This is a **second concept for discovery**, not the recommended first build or a claim to certify disability.

## 2. Closest prior art and implementation choices

| System | Already demonstrated or documented | Consequence for this product |
|---|---|---|
| **AnonCreds** | Holder link secrets, blinded issuance, selective disclosure, predicates and private non-revocation are established specification features. The BC government reports using Aries/AnonCreds and BC Wallet in credential use cases. | A real issuer/wallet ecosystem exists. Our custom signature/circuit is not an AnonCreds implementation or a drop-in importer. Prefer its mature machinery if standard credential exchange, rather than EVM experimentation, is the objective. |
| **Privado ID / iden3** | Issuer node, holder wallet/SDK, credential revocation and EVM verification. Official docs explicitly describe checking issuer/user state and executing custom business logic in the same transaction. | “Private qualification unlocks payment” is already a documented integration pattern. Embedded versus universal verifiers also force a decision about reusable verification state and linkability. |
| **Semaphore v4** | Anonymous group membership and scoped nullifiers, with Solidity and JS tooling. | If qualification is simply membership in a current roster, this may be sufficient. A custom signature plus non-revocation circuit needs a reason beyond code novelty: credential lifecycle/attributes and issuer workflow must justify it. |
| **ShadowPath** | August 2026 preprint: holder-local status lookup, authenticated registry roots and hidden status paths; compares sparse Merkle and Verkle costs. Its paper explicitly describes an integrated workflow rather than a new primitive. | Direct inspiration, not a novelty blank cheque. Published assumptions exclude malicious issuer behavior, issuer–verifier collusion and synchronization privacy. The artifact is not a production anonymous-credential integration. |
| **USENIX 2026 Private Record Certification** | Peer-reviewed PRC construction using PIR/MPC, with decentralized revocation authorities; official venue identifies a Distinguished Artifact Award. Authors report sub-second requests at billion-record scale. | Stronger research evidence for an alternative status architecture. Those measurements are the authors', not our benchmarks; this is not a ready-made BN254/Fuji verifier or adoption evidence for this application. |

Primary sources: [AnonCreds specification](https://anoncreds.github.io/anoncreds-spec/); [BC government implementation procurement](https://marketplace.digital.gov.bc.ca/opportunities/code-with-us/d81f1bb4-4abb-4a4c-8b19-92904e0bd6a6); [Privado onchain verification](https://docs.privado.id/docs/verifier/on-chain-verification/overview/); [Privado issuer guide](https://docs.privado.id/docs/issuer-node/issuer-node-guide/); [Semaphore overview](https://pse.dev/projects/semaphore); [ShadowPath full preprint, 20 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19937v1); [USENIX paper page, August 2026](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/edalatnejad). All accessed 12 September 2026.

The [PRC full paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity26-edalatnejad.pdf) makes a crucial distinction: privacy tolerates up to all-but-one malicious revocation authorities, while its unforgeability guarantee does not tolerate actively malicious authorities; it explicitly notes that one can forge non-revocation tokens. This is a scalability/trust tradeoff, not uniformly stronger security. Its [published artifact](https://zenodo.org/records/20433788) benchmarks PRC in Docker; credential-presentation and equality-proof costs are separate. A new application would still need that credential composition and deployment authority model.

Adoption claims need care. Privado's HSBC page describes a **prototype**, not a proven production banking deployment. Its Telefónica announcement describes systems integration and proofs of concept, including certification use cases. Its credential marketplace returned a network-error page to the web reader; that does not prove the service is universally down, but it is not evidence of a usable qualification issuer inventory. ([HSBC prototype](https://www.privado.id/blog/why-hsbc-is-building-a-decentralized-identity-solution-with-polygon-id), [Telefónica announcement](https://www.privado.id/blog/telefonica-tech-partners-with-privado-id-to-drive-adoption-of-private-compliant-digital-identities), [marketplace](https://marketplace.privado.id/))

A more current deployment claim comes from [Billions' 6 February 2026 RedChain case study](https://billions.network/blog/case-study-spanish-red-cross-runs-privacy-first-aid-platform-with-billions-zk-technology): it reports Spanish Red Cross/BLOOCK using iden3-based eligibility credentials in aid operations. This supports the practical relevance of minimizing disclosure around financial eligibility, but is vendor-reported evidence, not an independently audited privacy result. Its transaction/validation totals must not be interpreted as credential-holder counts or our potential market. It is additional prior art against claiming that credential-gated financial action is itself new.

**The exact possible new contribution** is a small, independently usable *application protocol*: one sound proof binds an issuer-authenticated class, hidden non-revocation index, holder secret and expiry to a particular paid assignment and beneficiary; credential-independent state distribution survives the original UI; fresh-root checking, duplicate acceptance and subsequent payment rights are explicitly reconciled. A second client can run the same workflow. The contribution is in this demonstrable combination and its application semantics, not inventing anonymous credentials, revocation, nullifiers, encrypted storage or a proof-gated token transfer.

Even that is a hackathon integration contribution, not yet a defensible moat. If the demonstration is only a credential-wallet screen followed by an airdrop, the correct verdict is **framework reskin**. The custom BN254/depth-16 design is also a restricted format with a trusted setup, not interoperability with existing credentials. Proving its own relation does not establish the truth of an issuer's assessment.

## 3. Nine-input interface privacy review

The interface exposes issuer key X/Y, current registry root, qualification class, proof deadline, job context, payout recipient, job-scoped nullifier and a presentation tag. Its private witness includes the signed credential and the authentication path at that same signed index. These comments are requirements and deductions from the interface, not a circuit audit or a claim that all checks currently pass.

| Boundary | Required behavior and residual exposure |
|---|---|
| **Issuer–verifier collusion** | If the holder chooses a strong secret locally, knowledge of its commitment should not directly reveal secret-derived nullifiers under the intended hash assumptions. Do not mechanically inherit ShadowPath's different identifier-binding guarantee. Still, a malicious issuer can isolate people using unique classes, cohorts, selective revocation or tailored roots; its identity records plus timing/wallet information can correlate them. No broad collusion-resistance claim is justified. |
| **Wallet linkability** | Recipient and acceptance/payment transactions are public. Reusing the same wallet links jobs independently of the proof. Fresh addresses alone do not defeat gas-funding, withdrawal or network correlation. The accurate claim is hidden credential index/serial, not an untraceable employment history. |
| **Scoped pseudonym** | Derive the nullifier only from holder secret and domain-bound job context. It must stay stable for repeat applications to one job, even after changing recipient/deadline; it should change for another job. Do not expose that value as a permanent reviewer profile or public history key. |
| **One secret versus one person** | Nullifiers stop reuse of a secret for one job. Multiple credentials under different secrets evade a “one human” rule unless issuance policy handles uniqueness and replacement. Software possession does not prevent voluntary secret sharing or prove that the human who passed an assessment is physically doing the work. |
| **Witness and key handling** | Issuer receives a commitment, not the holder secret. A hosted prover that receives the witness can identify the credential. A local CLI/helper is a valid experiment but must be labeled; it is not browser-local proving. Never put plaintext credentials, recovery keys or witnesses in logs, URLs, Arkiv attributes or Swarm gateway requests. Encrypt before upload. |
| **Synchronization** | Fetch a shared issuer snapshot/epoch object and reconstruct locally; avoid a request containing this holder's index. The server still sees network metadata and which issuer/cohort was fetched. A content hash gives integrity, not anonymous retrieval. |
| **Freshness** | Contract checks its configured root and key, not whichever root the client submits. That prevents stale-root acceptance relative to configured state; it does not compel an issuer to publish a revocation promptly. Display issuer state/block/time and a separate proof deadline. |
| **Narrow cohorts** | Public class, issuer, deadline and state changes may narrow the anonymity set to one. Use coarse classes and shared short validity windows where possible. A two-holder demonstration cannot support a real-world anonymity-set claim. |
| **Revocation and payment** | A later revocation blocks new qualification-based acceptance. It must not erase an already established payment obligation. Specify precisely when work becomes earned or approved and who resolves nonperformance. Revocation is not a retroactive refund oracle. |

The strongest adversarial demonstrations are therefore: copied proof to another job/contract/chain fails; edited beneficiary fails; a second application with another payout address fails; revoked or expired credentials fail; an old root fails after rotation; an already-approved payment still releases after revocation. Show verifier-visible fields and network requests, not only a green badge. Repeated proof byte differences alone do not establish unlinkability.

## 4. Product and sponsor composition

**Arkiv:** public, expiring work opportunities and issuer-signed shared state announcements; compound queries by task class, client and availability; real WSS updates. Expiry removes opportunities from discovery. Monetary deadlines and accepted assignments remain enforceable onchain. Never treat public Arkiv entities as secret credentials or use disappearance as authority to invalidate earned funds.

**Swarm:** client-encrypted holder export and confidential review artifacts, plus public authenticated issuer-state snapshots where appropriate. A second client retrieves and verifies actual bytes. Private object references can still be correlated. Give the holder an explicit export/recovery path; loss of the decryption key remains a loss unless a separately designed recovery mechanism exists. Revoking access cannot retract plaintext already downloaded.

**Avalanche:** actual Fuji test-stablecoin funding, qualification-bound acceptance, client approval and exactly specified settlement/refund rules. This is useful financial enforcement, not decoration. A client must not be able to manufacture a revocation to cancel a debt; the issuer and payer are distinct roles even if team-owned test keys operate both during the demo.

**ENS:** optional issuer-administration roles on genuine ENSv2 beta contracts only if they simplify a real task. A display name alone is not meaningful. Sepolia ENS state does not automatically authorize Fuji issuer-key changes. Omit it before adding an unexplained cross-chain trust adapter.

## 5. Go/no-go gates

**Proceed with the bounded experiment** if genuine issuer-signature/non-revocation composition, current-root rejection, beneficiary binding and payment lifecycle all run; browser/CLI boundaries are disclosed; a second client can independently verify and retrieve; sponsor writes/retrievals are real; and the demonstration makes one meaningful attempted abuse fail.

**Do not claim commercial viability** until one issuer agrees to issue its own attestations and two independent clients agree that they want the minimized disclosure and will honor them. Do not launch a marketplace to hide the absence of this relationship. The most defensible initial distribution is an issuer-led pilot with existing client relationships, not recruiting holders and buyers separately from zero.

Ask prospective participants, when outreach is separately authorized:

- Issuer: what exact property do you certify, how do you revoke/reissue it, and would you attest it without learning every later client?
- Client: what documents/identifiers do you currently request, which are actually necessary, and what would you accept from this named issuer instead?
- Holder: which current disclosure is harmful or burdensome, and would keeping only that field private matter despite a public payment address?
- Both businesses: who handles identity, tax, confidentiality and dispute obligations, and when is payment irrevocably earned?

**Kill or narrow** if clients require the same stable identity anyway, if the issuer will not integrate, if ordinary server pseudonyms meet the entire need, if proving exposes the witness to the verifier's backend, if only a singleton cohort is available, or if the prototype's revocation authority can erase a completed job's payment. A successful cryptographic probe remains useful evidence even if the product decision is no-go.

# Additional research that changed the shortlist

Research date: 12 September 2026, Europe/Bucharest. This supplements the independent frontier, winner and sponsor reports. Links below are primary publications, project documentation or firsthand product descriptions. Product marketing is evidence of a claimed capability and competitor positioning, not independent verification of performance or adoption.

## Private, verifiable reads: excellent problem, existing direct implementations

The user problem is real: querying a wallet can expose the addresses a person cares about to the RPC provider. Ethereum's [privacy roadmap](https://ethereum.org/roadmap/privacy/) includes private reads. Its [July 2 wallet article](https://ethereum.org/latest/next-great-wallet-private) is useful background **outside** the requested two-month window. A private query, network anonymity, answer correctness and answer freshness are separate guarantees.

The August 10 research report [Single-Server Verifiable PIR with Updates and Universally Composable Security](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1651) studies private information retrieval with authorized updates and consistency. It is a useful reason to distrust a demo that privately retrieves an unauthenticated stale balance. It does not establish that a production-ready browser implementation of that construction is available.

Direct prior art removes the easy pitch. [Orochi's private-eth-getbalance](https://github.com/orochi-network/private-eth-getbalance) documents browser WASM private balance queries, live Ethereum ingestion and reconciliation. [Poulpy PIR](https://github.com/poulpy-fhe/poulpy-pir) implements InsPIRe and points to a separate Ethereum token-balance application. Its Rust library uses selectable backends; the example performance environment should not be mistaken for guaranteed laptop/browser performance. Neither repository was compiled or benchmarked in this round.

[SimplePIR](https://github.com/ahenzinger/simplepir) and [VeriSimplePIR](https://github.com/leodec/VeriSimplePIR) remain relevant earlier building blocks. SimplePIR's benchmark shortcuts and preprocessing costs matter; copying a throughput figure without those conditions would be misleading. Ethereum's [August 18 allocation report](https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/08/18/allocation-q2-26) independently shows active funding in private reads. This is an ecosystem to contribute to, not an unoccupied idea.

**Decision:** do not build “private wallet balance on Avalanche” as the pivot. A worthwhile contribution would need a specific application and a real improvement in authenticated freshness, supported queries or usability, with performance measured here. Arkiv/ENS/Swarm/Fuji integration alone would not supply that improvement.

## Conditional exchange: good consumer story, limited mechanism novelty

[Policies for Fair Exchanges of Resources](https://lmcs.episciences.org/18805) was published in LMCS on **20 July 2026**, accepted June 22 and originally submitted in 2024. MuAC describes exchange conditions; MuACL supplies a decidable fairness relation. The article already sketches blockchain NFT exchange. Its model also leaves temporal behavior and changing policies as limitations. A product could explore explicit deadlines and stale-consent rejection, but should credit the authors and avoid claiming to have implemented their full logic merely by checking a three-edge cycle.

The [Barterplace thesis](https://dspace.lib.ntua.gr/xmlui/bitstream/handle/123456789/63123/Thesis.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) and the more recent journal work discussed in [winners.md](winners.md) overlap multiparty NFT barter; search results also include an unrelated same-name MultiversX repository. Do not conflate those implementations. [NeoSwap's own 2022 litepaper](https://medium.com/neoswap/neoswap-litepaper-dbf5d383632e) already describes multi-item, multi-party exchange with NFTs and currency. [ShiftHawk](https://shifthawk.ai/) markets multi-person shift cycles with rest/role constraints and existing-calendar imports; its founder's August post is supporting firsthand context, not an independent adoption study.

**Decision:** SWITCH survives as a product-design alternative, not a new trading algorithm. An actual issuer must honor reassigned rights, and the system needs useful preference overlap. The strongest possible difference is private conditional consent plus enforceable reassignment across independently controlled issuers. A synthetic booking token cannot establish that. These access and market-density dependencies make it a weaker immediate bet than the recovery prototype.

## Durable recovery: the nearest competitors and the remaining contribution

[Restate](https://docs.restate.dev/foundations/key-concepts) explicitly uses payment followed by receipt delivery to explain journaled recovery. It already handles retries, multiple instances and stale processes. [DBOS](https://www.dbos.dev/blog/scaleable-decentralized-workflows) describes workers coordinating through a shared database without a central orchestration process; that article dates October 16, 2025 and is foundational prior art. [Dapr Agents](https://dapr.github.io/dapr-agents/concepts/agents/) also documents persistent workflow state and fault-tolerant execution. There is no defensible claim that onchain idempotency invents durable agents.

Cross-tool continuation is also occupied. [Handover's August 3, 2026 Continuity Record v1.0](https://handover.sh/research) is a platform-neutral format with an explicit separation from transport, authentication and storage. Its small authored benchmark and internal migration report have stated limitations. [Zephr Slipstream](https://zephr.ai/en/product/slipstream) advertises signed, destination-bound handoffs and a consumption ledger. These are direct reasons not to claim invention of portable signed context. We inspected their public descriptions, not their entire implementations.

The initial working name BATON also collided with [Agent Baton Protocol](https://github.com/ziyad-m97/agent-baton-protocol), an existing TON delegation/escrow project. Its README distinguishes some implemented settlement components from a simulated render worker. It is neither our work nor a verified competitor benchmark. **Use REPRISE as a provisional internal name instead.** This name screen is not trademark clearance; the product proposal remains independent of branding.

The July 27 [PACT draft](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-laxsharma-pact-00.html) further overlaps contracts for paid agent work. A signed scope, escrow and a receipt are not sufficient novelty. [Cashfree's Relay](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/ai-superagent-that-runs-smb-payment-operations/) markets AI payment operations with approval gates and a shared first-party execution system. Its page displayed a relative recent date when inspected; this report does not manufacture an exact publication day or repeat its performance numbers as independent evidence.

**Remaining product hypothesis:** the owner can replace the operator of a narrowly supported paid job after losing access to that operator and its journal, using previously published encrypted inputs and independently enforced financial state. The surviving authorization, supported side effects, key distribution and operator compensation must be explicit. This is a composition and interoperability contribution; it is neither a novel consensus algorithm nor proof that an arbitrary third-party business action ran exactly once. A two-operator cold recovery is the required evidence.

## Research-heavy alternatives: keep actual dependencies visible

[Prezta](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/wei-zhongjing), presented at USENIX Security in August, demonstrates client-side zkVM authorization with inexpensive verification and substantial proving overhead. Its reported conformance is partial. It suggests a worthwhile architectural pattern for constrained verifiers but does not make a proof mandatory for a contract that can directly check a short payment instruction.

[ShadowPath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19937), submitted August 20, is more directly relevant to privately proving current qualification. A temporary-work product could demonstrate valid, revoked, stale and replayed presentations. The hard gates are a runnable prover, a real issuer/verifier relationship and a privacy model that includes linkable payments. None was implemented in this round.

[EMVCo's September 1 framework announcement](https://www.emvco.com/news/emvco-requests-feedback-on-framework-for-secure-interoperable-and-scalable-card-based-agentic-payments/) supports the relevance of scoped agent payment authorization, but it is a consultation draft and is not a crypto-product endorsement. The [August 15 attested-payment Internet-Draft](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-hawkins-scitt-attested-agent-payment-01.html) distinguishes attestations and authorization. A hardware-backed key alone does not prove reviewed code executed. Avoid a TEE pitch until actual attestation can run in the available environment.

These leads are retained as alternatives rather than silently discarded. No general-purpose agent marketplace, new token or novel cryptography is required for the recommended bounded recovery experiment.

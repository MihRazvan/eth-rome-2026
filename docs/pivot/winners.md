# What the prior winners teach us—and three different products worth testing

Research date: 12 September 2026. Author: independent product/design research worker. This report answers the hard-pivot request; it does not assume EXIT or withdrawal markets remain the product. Concepts below are proposals, not implemented capabilities, verified demand, or claims of worldwide invention.

**Recommendation:** carry forward the team's ability to make a precise trust boundary visible, rather than its previous product categories. Of the consumer alternatives researched here, **SWITCH** has the strongest demonstration, **ALL IN** has the clearest payment job, and **COUNTERCLAIM** has the most immediate adversary but the weakest novelty. Late discovery of ShiftHawk and BarterSwap materially weakens SWITCH's novelty: it is an issuer-integration hypothesis, not an unexplored market. Compare these against the integrator's independently researched trust primitives before committing. None of these should displace a stronger idea merely to cover another bounty.

## 2. ANYWARE: a surprising result with a visible chain of evidence

The product proposition is unusually compressible: **a destination contract can inspect a fact on another chain without trusting a courier's statement about that fact**. Its [README](https://github.com/MihRazvan/ETH_Prague_Hackathon/blob/43bb5dcb788c5c55214f9a6824fea3c8f205b997/README.md) and [demo script](https://github.com/MihRazvan/ETH_Prague_Hackathon/blob/43bb5dcb788c5c55214f9a6824fea3c8f205b997/docs/DEMO_SCRIPT.md) put a financial action around that primitive: lock collateral on Sepolia, assemble a proof, borrow demo stablecoins on Base Sepolia, then repay and settle.

Why that is compelling, as an inference from the artifacts:

- There is one apparent impossibility to resolve. Assets stay in one place while their state becomes useful elsewhere.
- Each technical layer has a narrative role: the source creates the fact, an untrusted courier collects evidence, and the destination checks it.
- The user sees an outcome—receiving purchasing power—not just a hash or SDK response.
- The proof chain can be drawn as a ladder. Judges can ask where trust enters and inspect the answer.
- The repository supports multiple depths: interactive transaction flow, technical playground, extensive explanation, SDK, Solidity consumer example and an educational game.

The [actual verifier](https://github.com/MihRazvan/ETH_Prague_Hackathon/blob/43bb5dcb788c5c55214f9a6824fea3c8f205b997/contracts/src/BeaconStateProof.sol) reads an EIP-4788 root, reconstructs a beacon header, checks an execution-header SSZ branch, traverses an account proof and a storage proof, and applies an optional timestamp freshness bound. The [interactive demo](https://github.com/MihRazvan/ETH_Prague_Hackathon/blob/43bb5dcb788c5c55214f9a6824fea3c8f205b997/webapp/pages/demo.tsx) contains wallet, chain, proof latency, transaction and balance state. This is more than a pitch deck. However, `ProofVisualizer.tsx` is explanatory static step copy; its presence alone does not prove every displayed step was independently instrumented. Likewise the game's explicit `ADVANCE` state machine makes a useful teaching metaphor, not a second chain-verification implementation.

There are consequential boundaries to avoid repeating:

1. **Evidence of a historical lock is not enforcement of a loan.** [Vault.unlock](https://github.com/MihRazvan/ETH_Prague_Hackathon/blob/43bb5dcb788c5c55214f9a6824fea3c8f205b997/contracts/src/demo/Vault.sol#L32) checks the local lock status and returns ETH; it does not enforce remote repayment. [Lender.borrow](https://github.com/MihRazvan/ETH_Prague_Hackathon/blob/43bb5dcb788c5c55214f9a6824fea3c8f205b997/contracts/src/demo/Lender.sol#L67) can close an old demo loan while burning only the balance available. This is a proof demonstration with deliberately simplified credit, not evidence of production-secured lending.
2. **Inclusion and freshness should not be described as independently proven finality.** The inspected entry point has no explicit finality-proof verification. [EIP-4788](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4788) exposes parent beacon block roots; the stronger README wording about a finalized execution block needs its own argument. This is a source-level scope observation, not a complete audit or demonstrated exploit.
3. **Untrusted offchain computation still needs availability.** RPC/beacon endpoints, correct serialization/layout assumptions and compatible destination infrastructure remain operational dependencies.
4. The demo script explicitly provides a pregenerated-proof contingency. That is a sensible presentation fallback only when labeled; it is not evidence of fresh proving under present conditions.

What to reuse: the method of making a trust boundary executable, visualizing the evidence chain, and connecting a primitive to a tangible result. What not to sell as new: EIP-4788 state proofs, another proof SDK, the same bridge-free borrowing illustration, or a reskin of the winter/source-to-summer/destination educational journey.

## 3. AUTARK: the attacker is understandable before the architecture is

AUTARK begins with a concrete threat: **one compromised deployment credential can replace the website users trust**. Its answer moves publication authority into a threshold-controlled release process. The [current README](https://github.com/MihRazvan/Autark/blob/481d4d23e887d5f1912c1d82a4d71c6979f36216/README.md) gives a complete operational path: build, upload through Storacha to IPFS, derive the next ENS version, create a Safe proposal, review, approve and publish; later channel promotion and rollback are explicit workflows.

The important product move was identifying *the exact action an attacker must be unable to perform*. The proposed demo can attempt a malicious release with one signer, show it waiting, then show an authorized release change a visible website. That is easier to evaluate than “decentralized DevOps.” It also turns infrastructure into indispensable roles: Safe controls approval, content addressing identifies the exact artifact, ENS supplies the public name/pointer, and the CLI fits the existing build workflow.

The inspected implementation supports that story in concrete ways. [`safe-batch-deploy.ts`](https://github.com/MihRazvan/Autark/blob/481d4d23e887d5f1912c1d82a4d71c6979f36216/src/lib/ens/safe-batch-deploy.ts) encodes subnode creation plus contenthash assignment as one Safe batch for a Safe-owned parent. The architecture also admits a different personally owned-parent path. Config/auth/error work and deploy/promote/rollback commands show integration discipline, rather than only contract novelty.

Do not inherit the broadest guarantees without testing them. The selected [`fuses.ts` policy](https://github.com/MihRazvan/Autark/blob/481d4d23e887d5f1912c1d82a4d71c6979f36216/src/lib/ens/namewrapper/fuses.ts) burns `CANNOT_SET_RESOLVER`. ENS documents this as preventing a change of **resolver contract**; it does not by itself say that the owner's records inside that resolver can never change. ENS also documents expiry-related limits to fuse permanence. The content-addressed artifact, the authorization to change its pointer, and storage retention are separate properties. Thus unconditional claims of immutable records or eternal availability are not established by this inspected fuse selection. ([ENS fuse documentation](https://docs.ens.domains/wrapper/fuses/))

The lesson is not that the project lacks value: threshold-controlled publication is meaningful even when the naming-record immutability story needs tightening. It is that the next demo should prove its strongest claim with an attempted forbidden action. Two signature prompts are not proof that every publishing path has the same enforcement.

What not to repackage: Safe-governed publishing, ENS versioned deployment pointers, IPFS/Swarm substitution for hosted builds, “unruggable websites,” or the same CI approval mechanism with agents replacing developers. Both inspected repositories use MIT licenses; copied code still needs attribution, and event novelty is separate from license permission.

## 4. The reusable team advantage—and the current novelty trap

The evidence supports five strengths: contract-plus-client delivery, cryptographic provenance, readable integration boundaries, demonstrations with visible state changes, and documentation that lets another builder reproduce the core idea. It does **not** establish customer acquisition, production economic security, production privacy, or an advantage in training models.

A good next pitch has this shape: **“This person used to be able to do X to you. Try it now. The system refuses because Y is checked by a party they cannot silently control.”** That is a design criterion, not a reason to force every product into adversarial security.

There is a fresh collision check: Arkiv's own [August 2026 ideathon board](https://ideathon.arkiv.network/) already lists expiring negative agent memory, agent capability checks, MCP attestations, transitive delegation revocation, causal graphs, disclosure deadlines, warranty records, anti-double-sale listings and approval-risk graphs. Its entries include *Airlock*, *CausalGraph*, *Tried That* and *MCP Attest*. These are published ideas, not all demonstrated production systems. Nevertheless, calling the same pattern novel without identifying the material difference is especially weak in front of the sponsor who judged them.

Generic agent marketplaces also need funded buyers, task verification, liability and acquisition. Generic portable memory needs selective disclosure, deletion boundaries and a reason users move between providers. Storing JSON on Swarm and indexing it on Arkiv answers neither problem. Reject claims that expiration erases copies or that an AI verdict turns a subjective dispute into cryptographic truth.

## 5. Three substantially different concepts

### A. SWITCH — “Everyone gets the time they need, or nobody loses their place.”

**Product and initial user.** A closed group exchanging scarce, issuer-recognized reservations: start with workshop sessions or community facility bookings, not hospitals or regulated employment. A holder says which replacement slots they would accept and when that consent expires. The app discovers a cycle; every holder and the issuer authorize one exact reassignment. All rights change together or none do. Participants never surrender their current place merely to join a waitlist.

**Evidence and distinction.** Microsoft's current Shifts workflow explicitly requires a coworker's acceptance and then managerial approval for a bilateral swap. When I Work also documents employee-selected acceptable replacement shifts. These establish a real coordination job and substantial incumbents, not willingness to adopt blockchain. ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/shifts/request-open-shifts-swap-or-offer-shifts-in-shifts), [When I Work](https://help.wheniwork.com/articles/getting-your-shifts-covered/)) Trading cycles are old market-design work, acknowledged in [Alvin Roth's 2012 Nobel lecture slides](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/roth-lecture_slides.pdf). The candidate innovation is a portable, consent-bound execution surface across independent reservation interfaces—not inventing matching or tokenized tickets.

**Late, decisive collision.** [ShiftHawk](https://shifthawk.ai/) currently advertises three-to-five-person shift cycles, role/rest checks, calendar imports, request acceptance and local processing. Its public site links app stores, but this reviewer did not install or test it. This is much closer than the bilateral incumbents and substantially weakens a generic shift-swap pitch. [BarterSwap's authors](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404571129_BarterSwap_A_TTC-Based_Protocol_for_Multi-Party_NFT_Exchange_without_Monetary_Transfers/download) describe TTC-based multiparty NFT exchange on Ethereum (2026, DOI `10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3690708`), and [NeoSwap's September 2022 litepaper](https://medium.com/neoswap/neoswap-litepaper-dbf5d383632e) already describes multiparty NFT/currency trades. Consequently, SWITCH should proceed only if recognized issuer policy and independent execution supply a user benefit those existing mechanisms do not; adding tokens to a shift finder is insufficient.

**Legitimate issuer and narrow launch.** The most credible first inventory is a community operator's own workshop or technical-review appointments, with a written decision that the consumption check follows the reassigned right. The team could supply its own real review-session slots only if it actually commits to honor them; that would be a small real service, not an integration with someone else's calendar. An existing venue or event is a prospective partner until it explicitly opts in. A demo NFT named after a real venue without that agreement remains synthetic inventory. Consumption should check the final holder and prevent double redemption; an issuer cancellation/refund policy must be visible.

**Closer protocol prior art.** [CoW Protocol](https://docs.cow.fi/) already batches intents to exploit coincident wants. [Seaport](https://docs.opensea.io/docs/seaport) already supplies an NFT marketplace settlement protocol, so atomic exchange of tokens is not a new primitive. The [MuAC/MuACL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21214), first posted 28 October 2024, explicitly models policy-respecting resource exchanges and application to blockchain NFTs; the integrator identified its later 2026 journal publication. Do not borrow its formal fairness claim for an ad hoc cycle finder. The useful new behavior must live in issuer constraints, participant consent, private preference handling and the recognized redemption path. Kidney exchange and classical trading cycles are conceptual predecessors, not evidence that ordinary booking rights can be freely reassigned.

**90-second demonstration.** In 0–20 seconds, show three people stuck with Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday reservations; none has a bilateral match. In 20–40, reveal a three-person cycle satisfying all preferences. In 40–60, attempt a changed destination or reuse one participant's expired consent: the whole operation fails and all retain their places. In 60–80, gather fresh approvals and execute; three independently inspected ownership records change atomically. In 80–90, open the receipt from a second client and show the issuer's policy and exact reassignment.

**Sponsor architecture.** Arkiv indexes signed active replacement intents and streams changes over genuine WSS. An expired intent must disappear from matching without a deletion worker; contract deadlines separately protect execution. Swarm holds encrypted detailed availability and portable, signed assignment receipts. Public schedules and personal reasons should not be written into query attributes. An ENSv2 subregistry can give an institution distinct scheduling and issuer-management roles; demonstrate those role limits on Sepolia if included. Avalanche Track B is natural only if tokenized reservation rights, eligibility and any actual exchange payment/bond settle on Fuji. ENS authorization on another chain must not be hand-waved into Fuji policy: either keep the named identity supplementary, explicitly trust a disclosed adapter, or omit one integration.

**Hard boundary.** A chain cannot make a venue honor a booking. The issuer must recognize the ledger as authoritative. No hidden requirement for a solver to be honest: the contract validates every edge and exact consent, while a malicious solver can at most omit a solution. Metadata, holders and eventual transfers remain public unless a separately verified privacy design changes that.

**Validation/kill gate.** Obtain one operator's acceptance of an authoritative pilot inventory and test at least three real preference cycles. Kill the external-operator claim if this is merely tokenizing a spreadsheet the operator will ignore. Kill the decentralization thesis if all authority and participants are in one trusted scheduler and an ordinary database transaction meets every requirement. The interface and algorithm can still be useful, but that becomes a different business.

### B. ALL IN — “Nobody fronts the money. Nobody changes the deal.”

**Product and initial user.** A community organizer arranging a shared booking from one or several willing suppliers. Every participant approves a concrete plan hash: suppliers, dates, per-person maximum, minimum group, delivered booking rights, refund policy and deadline. Funds are deposited against that version. Finalization must satisfy the complete plan and mint/transfer the agreed rights; the organizer cannot redirect a paid-up group to a different beneficiary or silently increase its price. Changed terms need new consent. If the plan never closes, contributors can recover their own balances.

**Evidence and distinction.** WeTravel sells payment collection and financial administration for group travel, including custom trips and retreats; this demonstrates an existing purchasing workflow and entrenched alternatives. Kickstarter documents unsuccessful payment collection and a processing interval after funding success. Neither fact proves this audience wants stablecoins. ([WeTravel](https://help.wetravel.com/en/articles/253921-how-it-works), [Kickstarter](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010120934-If-my-project-is-successfully-funded-how-do-I-receive-my-funds)) More directly, [Party Protocol](https://github.com/PartyDAO/party-protocol) already provides group coordination and buy/auction crowdfunding. Therefore “pool money onchain” has no novelty. The defensible scope is exact multi-supplier consumer consent plus per-person fulfillment and independently recoverable funds, rather than a shared NFT purchase or discretionary group treasury.

**90-second demonstration.** Three people commit 30 test units each to one specific plan. The organizer tries to replace the supplier with its own wallet; the signature mismatch rejects the change. The fourth person's commitment lets the original plan close: suppliers receive their exact amounts and each participant gets the agreed booking right. A second plan expires with one person missing; a participant recovers their deposit from a separate interface while the organizer is offline. Native Arkiv expiry simultaneously removes that plan from live discovery.

**Sponsor architecture.** Fuji stablecoin escrow and actual settlement are central, making Team1 Track A an honest fit. Arkiv supplies short-lived plan/commitment discovery and true subscriptions; Swarm stores the encrypted itinerary and immutable signed terms that contributors can retrieve independently. Onchain payment amounts, membership and timing should not be called private. ENSv2 is optional: delegated community booking roles only justify it if demonstrated, not merely named wallets.

**Hard boundary.** A booking token proves an obligation was issued, not that dinner, travel or an event will happen. If suppliers cannot provide an enforceable onchain delivery primitive, show payment coordination only and explicitly retain supplier performance risk. Deposits are locked capital; compare that cost and wallet friction with card authorizations. Withdrawal and deadline semantics must be explicit to prevent last-participant griefing; an immutable commitment period is different from a freely cancellable pledge.

**Validation/kill gate.** Have one organizer and one supplier walk an actual plan through the consent model. Test whether the organizer has recently fronted money or chased contributions and whether those costs exceed adoption friction. Kill if a shared payment link already solves the problem, if participants refuse prefunding, or if the “multi-supplier” feature exists only to manufacture complexity. One credible group is better evidence than a projected travel TAM.

### C. COUNTERCLAIM — “Dispute the scratch, not my whole deposit.”

**Product and initial user.** Equipment rentals between small independent operators and repeat borrowers. Both parties sign an encrypted check-in manifest and a maximum deposit. On return, the operator must identify a specific deduction, amount and evidence before a deadline. Undisputed value becomes recoverable immediately; only the disputed slice remains with an agreed adjudication process. Either party can export a portable evidence packet even if the rental interface disappears.

**Evidence and distinction.** Government tenancy-deposit guidance shows that deposit recovery can require evidence and third-party resolution, including when a landlord cannot be contacted. This is adjacent evidence of the general pain, not proof that a equipment-rental pilot has demand or that residential tenancy rules permit this product. ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection/disputes-and-problems)) [Kleros already supports partial payments and disputes over the remaining amount](https://docs.kleros.io/products/escrow/new-in-progress-kleros-escrow-tutorial). Do not claim partial escrow is an invention. The candidate product improvement is the bilateral condition record, itemized disputed slice, and an independent renter recovery path in one usable flow. Its novelty is lower than SWITCH's.

**90-second demonstration.** Rent a camera with a 100-unit deposit and sign the pre-existing scratch into the condition manifest. At return, the operator alleges 15 units of new damage. The borrower retrieves 85 immediately. Replace a photo in the evidence package: its commitment no longer matches. A named test adjudicator resolves the 15-unit remainder; the complete payout reconciles to 100. A separate expired no-claim rental returns its full deposit without the operator cooperating.

**Sponsor architecture.** Swarm has a strong purpose: encrypted evidence belongs to both parties and an explicitly chosen adjudicator, not the platform accused of suppressing it. Arkiv indexes active evidence requests/dispute-response availability, with native expiry and WSS; the settlement contract owns monetary deadlines independently. Avalanche escrow provides the financial enforcement. ENSv2 can constrain an adjudicator namespace, but an extra chain is optional rather than a prize-driven requirement.

**Hard boundary.** Hashes establish byte integrity, signatures establish who agreed, and timestamps constrain sequence. None proves that a scratch is real, who caused it, or the repair cost. AI may arrange evidence but must not silently decide financial liability. Expiration cannot delete already downloaded photographs. Avoid residential-deposit compliance claims; equipment borrowers and operators still need terms and a credible adjudicator.

**Validation/kill gate.** Interview an operator about the last three real deposit disputes, retained amounts, duration and current evidence process. Require a mutually acceptable reviewer and an operator willing to escrow funds. Kill if the operator will only join when it retains unilateral payout control, or if dispute cost exceeds deposits. Do not build another general-purpose escrow frontend and call it novel.

## 6. Selection and proof requirements

These are qualitative judgments, not estimated winning probabilities:

| Criterion | SWITCH | ALL IN | COUNTERCLAIM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memorable state change | Strong: impossible bilateral trade becomes a three-way cycle | Strong: tampered group deal rejected, original closes | Strong: undisputed money escapes dispute |
| Distance from prior team projects | High | High | High |
| Novelty after prior-art review | Low-medium; ShiftHawk and BarterSwap are direct collisions, issuer enforcement remains conditional | Medium-low; PartyDAO is a direct neighbor | Low-medium; Kleros already covers the core financial primitive |
| Main missing proof | Issuer adoption and real mutually acceptable cycles | Group willingness to prefund; supplier delivery | Operator participation and affordable adjudication |
| Natural sponsor center | Arkiv + Swarm; optional genuine rights settlement | Avalanche + Arkiv + Swarm | Swarm + Avalanche; Arkiv if response lifecycle matters |
| Reason to reject now | One trusted scheduler makes a database sufficient | Ordinary payment link solves the actual problem | General escrow reskin with a subjective oracle |

Current [ETHRome rules and bounty briefs](https://github.com/urbeETH/ethrome-2026-hacker-manual/blob/main/HACKER-MANUAL.md) permit multiple genuinely relevant sponsor entries and require disclosed provenance for existing work. Arkiv's expiry/live-subscription missions are natural for new builds; do not invent a former indexer to claim a migration. ENS requires actual ENSv2 beta functionality on Sepolia. Team1 requires real Fuji workflow evidence and one selected track. Swarm requires useful uploads/retrieval. Sponsor-specific live pages can supersede the mirrored manual; the integrator's current sponsor research is authoritative for payout figures and submission details. No aggregate prize estimate is used to rank these ideas.

Before selecting any concept, require four artifacts: a 90-second storyboard showing both a valid and forbidden action; a one-page authority model distinguishing cryptographic facts from trusted real-world assertions; a runnable probe of the hardest sponsor boundary; and a short record of the closest prior art plus the exact new behavior. For consumer concepts, add one concrete acquisition path and one falsifiable reason an actual person would adopt.

The next successful project should preserve the winners' clarity while improving their evidentiary discipline: fewer absolute claims, stronger negative tests, and one action that remains impossible even when the attractive interface is bypassed.

# EXIT demand, distribution and pivot assessment

Research date: **11 September 2026**. Independent skeptical assessment from repository base `da5e55de3e09d8a6ce50a23e491e433e9d35b18c`. This extends [the prior landscape](../research/market-landscape.md); it does not change the agreed implementation or admit a new source. No outreach, transactions or customer interviews were conducted. Operator reports establish reported use; they are not independently reconciled revenue statements. Source dates, retrieval limitations and contrary evidence are in [demand-sources.json](demand-sources.json).

## Decision

**Test a single-protocol embedded market for already queued transferable claims. Use an honest exit-check/comparison tool as a validation instrument, not an assumed standalone business. Do not yet finance a broad claim marketplace.** The evidence supports demand for timely liquidity and professional redemption arbitrage. It does not establish enough demand for buying someone else's already queued claim, independent maker participation, or willingness to pay EXIT specifically. Generic pre-queue liquid-token routing faces strong ARM/DEX incumbents.

The first distribution prospect is a protocol with recurring, healthy asynchronous withdrawals, native transferable claims and an existing pending-request screen. Lido withdrawal NFTs are a documented candidate; ether.fi withdrawal NFTs require the separate current-source admission review. The end user is a treasury operator or larger depositor whose cash deadline changed after entering the queue. The job is: “Show the executable ways to receive my required asset by my deadline, including their full cost; let me sell my supported remaining rights if that is the best available choice.” The buyer is a specialist redemption desk or explicitly funded partner treasury. These are proposed customer profiles, not discovered customers.

## What the evidence actually says

**Waiting is real; willingness to sell remains unmeasured.** ether.fi's June 9, 2026 operator report describes 542,792 ETH across 1,977 withdrawal requests during April 18–May 21. The reported median request-to-claimable time was 4.9 days, maximum 16.7 days. Its consolidation-based servicing reduced the need to use Ethereum's exit queue. This demonstrates substantial processed withdrawals and the issuer's ability to improve service itself. It neither counts unique customers nor measures users who would pay to escape an existing request. The original page contains an awkward sentence mixing “every request” with a median; this report uses its explicit summary statistics. [Operator report](https://www.ether.fi/blog/how-ether-fi-redeemed-20-percent-of-tvl-without-adding-to-exit-queue)

A firsthand Lido thread, resolved May 29, 2025, describes an estimated seven-day wait extending to roughly ten days and a countdown moving backward. The author ultimately reports successful withdrawal; a reply discusses estimate problems after Pectra. The identity of that respondent was not independently verified. This is useful evidence for status explanation and recovery, **not evidence of lost funds or a paid claim sale**. It also warns against interpreting frustrated posts without reading their resolution. [Complete user thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/LidoFinance/comments/1kxkah5/help_unstaking_with_lido_issues/)

**Operators already charge for speed.** ether.fi's April 25, 2025 governance proposal describes a 0.3% fast-withdrawal fee and separately captures yield forfeited during ordinary withdrawals. That is a concrete monetization mechanism and operator assertion of revenue, stronger than a generic complaint. It does not disaggregate fee-paying fills from forfeited yield or prove the proposal's execution. The old technical page now redirects to a missing-page notice; its search-index excerpt described buffer and rate limits, but a current executable quote was not obtained. Treat 30 bps as a historical comparison, not today's guaranteed price or availability. Never multiply all reported withdrawals by that fee. [Governance proposal](https://governance.ether.fi/t/11-ether-fi-dao-proposal-withdrawal-revenue-buyback-program/2920)

**Origin ARM is the strongest commercial comparable omitted from the earlier roundup.** Origin's July 30 article, updated August 12, 2026, describes a shared WETH vault quoting stETH, wstETH, eETH and weETH, recycling funds through Lido and ether.fi redemptions and lending idle capital. It reports more than $2.7 billion of indexed volume in the preceding stETH/eETH ARMs. This is cumulative routed trading, not unique demand, net revenue, or purchases of previously queued NFTs. The linked Dune page exposed only its shell here, so the figure remains operator-reported. Its separate $2.755 billion eligible 90-day market is explicitly an opportunity estimate, not ARM execution; do not merge the two. [Launch and methodology](https://www.originprotocol.com/blog/weth-arm-introduction), [linked query](https://dune.com/queries/8133533)

Origin's October 22, 2024 announcement says it supplied all initial ARM liquidity from December 2023 before opening to outside LPs. It identifies 1inch and CoWSwap integration as the source of trading flow independent of Origin's interface. Bootstrapping required capital and distribution before a broad LP invitation. [Original liquidity launch](https://www.originprotocol.com/blog/arm-vault?lang=en)

There are named capital-provider precedents: Origin's February 2026 eETH announcement names Lido Foundation, YO Protocol and Summer.fi as stETH ARM LPs. These are operator-attributed participants, not EXIT prospects who have agreed to participate. Yearn's own risk assessment, updated August 6, describes its curated Morpho allocation and a 20% ARM performance fee, giving a concrete example of professional diligence and fee architecture. Neither document proves that the same parties want bespoke claim inventory. ARM returns also include lending, so published APYs cannot be attributed entirely to sellers buying speed. [Origin announcement](https://www.originprotocol.com/blog/etherfi-arm-deposits), [Yearn assessment](https://github.com/yearn/risk-score/blob/master/reports/report/origin-arm.md)

**The same mechanism can fail a business test.** Origin's June 18, 2026 Sonic/OS ARM wind-down proposal says the products no longer clear its business, liquidity, revenue, risk and maintenance thresholds. It lists continuing redemption, monitoring, integration and support obligations. This is a primary statement that economic support was insufficient to justify operations on that deployment. The vote outcome and completed wind-down were not verified here; it is not evidence of insolvency or an exploit. A working contract, chain expansion and nominal yield are insufficient success criteria. [Wind-down proposal](https://governance.originprotocol.com/t/proposal-wind-down-origin-sonic-and-os-arm/227)

**Sanctum points toward distribution and a backstop, not an isolated receipt exchange.** Its December 8, 2023 operator report attributes almost $50 million cumulative volume to Jupiter/Solflare partnerships; the April 16, 2024 report claims more than $730 million through Jupiter. Those periods overlap and include broader LST flow, so they must not be added or called claim purchases. [Sanctum 2.0](https://sanctum.so/blog/sanctum-2-0-infinite-lst-future-on-solana), [Jupiter integration report](https://sanctum.so/blog/introducing-jupsol-liquid-staking-token-by-jupiter)

Current Sanctum Reserve documentation describes buying stake-account authority, waiting for deactivation, and replenishing SOL. It explicitly positions the reserve after Router/Infinity alternatives, restricts retail deposits, and documents utilization-dependent fees of 8–800 bps. These are documented bounds, not a checked executable quote. The important precedent is specialist backstop capital distributed through ordinary trading routes. Solana stake authority is not an EVM vault request. [Reserve mechanism](https://learn.sanctum.so/docs/technical-documentation/reserve)

## Three directions, with their hardest objection

| Direction | Concrete customer and job | Revenue and acquisition hypothesis | Hard objection / verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standalone queued-claim RFQ market | Holder already committed to a transferable request needs cash before finalization | Fee on completed sale; referrals from the originating protocol's pending-request screen | Arrives after many users chose a free wait; fragmented inventory, infrequent repeat use and two-sided cold start. Keep as supported execution capability, not an assumed marketplace business. |
| Exit comparison/routing | Depositor or treasury deciding before withdrawal, or checking an existing request | Protocol integration/support fee; disclosed execution fee only where outcome remains competitive | Lido, Origin and Sanctum already expose alternatives. Generic aggregation is easy to copy and often has no fee headroom. Best low-capital discovery surface if it adds source-state accuracy and supported claim routes. |
| Embedded liquidity service | Initially one protocol's already queued transferable claim holders; specialist buyer finances eligible flow | Pending-request-screen placement; test disclosed fill fees or an operator-paid service before assuming either | Issuer may prefer its own liquidity; distribution access and willingness to pay are unproven. Best narrow hypothesis only after holder, buyer and partner evidence align. |

Alternatives are state-dependent. Lido explicitly supports either selling liquid stETH or entering its withdrawal queue; after request creation, the holder owns an NFT rather than freely spendable stETH. An already claimable claim should compare directly with collection. A cancellable request may return shares that can then be swapped, but that sequence must be checked and priced; a theoretical DEX route for burned shares is not an alternative. [Lido withdrawal guide](https://lido.fi/how-lido-works/withdrawals)

Mellow is an example of a **future candidate class**, not the launch wedge, a validated integration or an underserved customer. Its stRATEGY documentation describes roughly 72-hour withdrawals paid in wstETH, with no withdrawal cancellation. Its Core Vault architecture already includes trusted-signature instant redemption queues. Ask why those existing mechanisms cannot meet the prospective partner's needs before proposing external liquidity. Do not infer request transferability from modular vault architecture. [stRATEGY flow](https://docs.mellow.finance/strategy-vault/overview), [Core architecture](https://docs.mellow.finance/core-vaults/architecture)

Avalanche is an implementation constraint, not proof of a market. EXIT's [pinned BENQI evidence](../evidence/benqi-source.md) finds caller-bound requests, a 15-day cooldown and two-day redemption window. Existing EOA requests cannot be imported by ERC20 approval. A partner would need to originate through the supported account beforehand; this makes embedded origination more credible than a rescue page for every person already waiting. That account remains a fork-validated prototype, not an enabled production market.

## Why a buyer funds it—and when they stop

A buyer funds an expected **net collection spread**, not a waiting animation:

`expected recovered proceeds − acquisition debit − funding cost − servicing/gas − venue fees − expected losses − required risk compensation`.

Illustration only: buying expected 10,000 proceeds for 9,960 creates 40 gross spread. At an assumed 8% simple annual capital hurdle, seven days consumes about 15.28; fourteen days consumes 30.56. With another 10 for execution/servicing/venue costs, the fourteen-day case is already slightly negative before loss risk. These are hypothetical inputs, not quoted funding rates or expected returns. Paying 5,985 for residual 6,000 leaves 15 gross; receiving 5,700 loses 285. The source can overwhelm the time-value spread.

The right first buyer already understands one source, holds the payout currency, can service and monitor claims, and has capital whose liabilities can tolerate delayed collection. A source-affiliated treasury may additionally value reliable customer exits, but any subsidy must be separately disclosed. A generic market maker quote API does not imply an appetite for multi-day, source-specific inventory.

Origin's September 4 risk disclosure makes the operational burden concrete: operators manage price bands and withdrawal batching; lending exposure adds withdrawal risk; LP redemptions have no maximum wait. Its deployment list differs from its July multi-asset announcement, another reason to inspect actual contracts before underwriting. No instant-liquidity promise should be built on redeeming another illiquid LP token. [Operator risk disclosure](https://www.originprotocol.com/blog/arm-risk-exposure)

Capital scales with inventory duration. Illustratively, funding 100,000 of purchases each day with a fourteen-day cash cycle ties up roughly 1.4 million before reserves or losses. Two signed makers are not two funded reserves if both rely on the same credit source. Under stress, source delays and demand for exits can rise together while makers pull bids. A resale button does not create a dependable secondary buyer.

Separate three cases in admission and analytics: **healthy wait**, where a reasonably measurable redemption exposure may be financeable; **operational hold**, where pause, oracle, custody or authorization failure needs diagnosis; and **loss/insolvency**, where the question is recoverable assets and priority, not simply days remaining. A larger discount transfers an exposure to a willing buyer; it does not repair the source. Distressed-claims underwriting is a different business and should not be the launch customer acquired by alarming social posts.

## Distribution, differentiation and privacy

Begin with one pending-request interface controlled by a willing protocol. An embedded “check this withdrawal” action can inspect existing claims, show direct collection or waiting, and request funded offers only for supported unfinalized rights. A holder arriving with an already claimable NFT should be helped to collect. The partner gets an additional exit option and auditable service metrics; a completed sale does not create retained AUM. Secure distribution access and separate maker capital before buying consumer traffic. Existing aggregators will not automatically route a bespoke claim NFT.

The protocol may refuse placement because discount quotes look like a solvency warning, support burden rises, or its own fast route earns more. Test this objection directly. An integration is not evidence that the issuer should subsidize trades or pay EXIT; identify the budget owner and the measurable benefit first. If embedding is unavailable, an independently linked claim inspector can recruit voluntary pilot users, but broad search traffic and “unstaking help” posts are likely to include unsupported claims and scams. Do not send unsolicited wallet-targeted offers. A maker-facing underwriting feed with claim state, servicing obligations and realized collections is the other half of distribution, not an automatic source of capital.

The defensible work would be verified source admission, reliable collection, source-specific pricing history, partner distribution and consistently funded execution. The current ownership/residual/privacy implementation is useful infrastructure, but not a commercial moat by itself. A copied screen or encryption layer is easier to reproduce than a trusted servicing relationship. New source integrations create ongoing liabilities; the Sonic proposal is a warning against expanding merely to display more logos.

Private offers have plausible value for larger negotiated tickets: competing makers need not see each other's unaccepted price or underwriting terms. 0x's documentation establishes private offchain RFQ alongside public settlement as an existing commercial pattern, but also says RFQ is selected only when competitive. This is not evidence of willingness to pay a privacy premium on withdrawal claims. [0x RFQ](https://help.0x.org/articles/3310442497-understanding-0x-rfq-request-for-quote)

Keep the required mode optional and test it with real makers. It cannot hide submitted settlement, guarantee anonymous demand, prevent a seller sharing bids, or create competing capital. With one subsidized maker it may add key/recovery friction without economic benefit. Evaluate maker participation, net price and seller completion with informed opt-in; do not export private competing quotes to analytics. Ask about actual lost trades or copied quotes before treating confidentiality as a purchase driver.

## Discovery and falsifiable gates

These are proposed experiments, not completed validation. No external messages were sent.

Ask recent withdrawers: “Show your last completed withdrawal and the alternatives available then. When did you need the cash? What did you actually pay? Why did you choose to queue? After requesting, what changed? At what exact net payment would you have switched?” Avoid asking whether an abstract fast-exit idea sounds useful.

Ask operators: “How many eligible requests arrived over the last 90 days, from how many unrelated customers? How many support cases were late estimates, failed claims or real cash deadlines? What do your buffer and current fast route cost? Who owns budget and interface placement? Would you pay a specified integration fee and expose a capped pilot?” Obtain aggregate evidence with appropriate permission, not user keys or private bid logs.

Ask prospective buyers: “What is your source limit, payout currency, capital hurdle and maximum lock duration? What would stop quoting? Price these actual eligible requests under normal, doubled-delay and loss scenarios. Can you provide capped independently controlled funds? Who services mature and exceptional claims?” A spreadsheet price without committed funds is not a maker.

| Gate | Proposed pass condition | Stop or narrow the direction when… |
|---|---|---|
| Historical eligibility | Review 90 days for one source; identify at least 20 unrelated eligible withdrawal episodes and classify authoritative request-to-claimable time | Most apparent delay is unclaimed available cash, unsupported rights, failed transactions or source loss |
| Distribution and budget | One partner commits interface placement, a responsible operator and a paid pilot or explicit budget; evidence comes from the decision-maker | Interest remains a hackathon introduction or unfunded letter of intent |
| Capital and price | Two independent potential buyers review the same source; at least one commits capped pilot capital and the other provides executable competitive quotes | Only team-funded makers quote, or buyers require a discount above observed seller tolerance |
| Paid demand | In a capped, separately authorized pilot, at least 5 of the first 20 qualified users accept an unsubsidized price, with at least 3 unrelated sellers | All interest disappears at the exact price; only rebates, points or distressed-loss cases produce trades |
| Realized economics | Reconcile at least 30 completed positions through collections; positive contribution after actual costs and a documented delay/loss stress test | Results rely on annualizing a lucky day, lending income counted as exit revenue, or perpetual partner subsidy |
| Privacy | At least 3 independent makers identify a concrete confidentiality need; optional private flow preserves completion and quote participation | No stated need beyond “privacy is good,” or recovery friction excludes the small maker set |

Thresholds are proposed operating gates, not statistically significant product-market-fit proof. Record denominators and unsuccessful requests. At an illustrative five-basis-point platform take, a 10,000 trade earns only 5 before support and infrastructure; covering 10,000 monthly fixed cost needs 20 million monthly settled notional. A modest profitable embedded service may therefore need a paid integration/maintenance component, rather than only tiny transaction fees.

The next investment should buy evidence: one source's observed exits, a paid distribution commitment and a funded buyer's loss-aware price. If those do not align, preserve the working settlement research and narrow to a comparison/support integration only when an operator will pay for it. More adapters and a public maker leaderboard would not resolve the missing commercial facts.

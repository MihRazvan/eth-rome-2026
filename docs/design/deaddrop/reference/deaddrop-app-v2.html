Five steps, and nobody ever meets

the accent cools as the work completes

  1. 01A collective assesses you

    It vouches for you against its own rubric and issues a credential you hold yourself. We never see it, and never store it.

  2. 02A client funds a task

    Public scope, escrowed USDC, and a listing that expires by itself. No claim by the deadline and the escrow refunds — nothing has to delete it.

  3. 03You cut a proof

    In your browser you prove the credential is valid and unrevoked, bound to this task and wallet. Single use, twenty-four hours, unlinkable to your other proofs.

  4. 04The report is sealed

    Encrypted to two keys and padded to a fixed size, so its length leaks neither scope nor severity. Stored on Swarm, its reference committed onchain.

  5. 05They open it and pay

    The client decrypts locally, reads it, and releases escrow to the address bound at acceptance. Nobody met. Nobody learned a name.

Three roles

everyone sees a different, deliberately incomplete picture

Collective

Assesses people its own way and issues a credential the holder keeps. Publishes signed status snapshots. Rules on disputes. Cannot see which holder claimed what, and cannot read any report.

Reviewer

Holds one credential. Cuts a single-use proof to claim a task. Seals the report to two keys. Gets paid to a public address.

Client

Funds a task, names the collectives it trusts, opens the report and approves payment. Learns that someone qualified did the work, never who.

Arkiv is the index, not the store: attributes carry what you query on, the bytes stay on Swarm. Arkiv is also not a confidentiality layer, so nothing identifying goes in an attribute — only commitments and hashes.

What we do not claim

the part most projects leave out

Not untraceable. Reusing one wallet across tasks links those tasks regardless of the proof. Use a fresh payout address per assignment if it matters.

Not one human, one credential. Multiple credentials under different secrets evade any such rule unless issuance handles uniqueness.

Not a quality guarantee. A proof says a collective approved someone in a class. It says nothing about whether this review is any good.

Not collusion-proof. A malicious collective can isolate a person with unique classes, tailored snapshots or selective revocation.

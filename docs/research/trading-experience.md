# EXIT trading experience: recovery before polish

Research and browser observation: **11 September 2026**, approximately 19:33–19:43 UTC. Research base: `b36fbf15e3f367caf225a1f8badc96c1449ea35b`. This assessment combines current firsthand interface observation, primary product documentation, accessibility guidance, and explicitly qualified community reports. It is an implementation decision aid, not investment advice or a claim of WCAG conformance.

The reviewer built the initial EXIT frontend in an earlier workstream. This is a fresh read-only critique of the integrated app, not an independent-author accessibility audit. The independent transport review already recorded in `docs/evidence/browser-independent.md` remains separate. Findings below were sent to the integrator before any new product edits.

## What the current product gets right

At `http://127.0.0.1:5173`, a new Chromium context loaded two actual local claims without a wallet. After the loading state resolved, claim #1 showed 10,000 expected and claim #2 showed 9,700 following its disclosed adverse scenario. No active offers were returned. Settlement was labeled connected; Arkiv and Swarm were unavailable. This session neither requested new offers nor signed, originated, collected, withdrew, cancelled, rotated keys, reset the chain, or changed time. Selecting a public local wallet only changed this browser's identity and triggered reads.

The receipt composition makes ownership more concrete than a generic balance card. Withdrawn cash is visibly outside remaining entitlement; detail separates pending proceeds, claimable proceeds, and cash still held. Claim #2 names the controlled loss and preserves the recovery-rights explanation. The seller ticket puts exact net, gross debit, fee, and gas treatment near the action. These are useful product strengths to preserve. Screenshot evidence is under [experience/](experience/); it is local-chain observation, not new transaction or sponsor verification.

## Sell now versus keep the claim

The exchange proposition is understandable, but the user must currently subtract the immediate offer from expected proceeds mentally. In the reference lifecycle, 9,960 now versus 10,000 expected implies a 40-unit difference. That difference is not automatically a fee, an expected loss, or a guaranteed return. A bounded future enhancement could show “40 below expected proceeds” beside the two alternatives and state the payout/timing assumptions. When everything is already claimable, the screen should visibly acknowledge that waiting may no longer be the main tradeoff; collection effort and gas can still matter. Source state must drive this explanation.

Pendle's primary PT documentation explicitly distinguishes the asset used to account for redemption from the underlying yield-bearing asset. Its market screen also pairs maturity duration with yield information. The transferable lesson is to name the unit and the condition that makes a comparison meaningful. EXIT's supported claims include uncertain timing, losses, and later recoveries, so importing Pendle-style fixed APY framing would overstate the source guarantee. Do not annualize the artificial test-vault timer into an investment proposition. [Pendle PT documentation](https://docs.pendle.finance/pendle-v2/ProtocolMechanics/YieldTokenization/PT), [observed Pendle markets](https://app.pendle.finance/trade/markets), accessed 2026-09-11.

Morpho's current public vault screen foregrounds browseable positions and differentiates deposits, liquidity, exposure, curator and yield. Its useful lesson is that quantity alone does not describe an asset's usability or risk. EXIT already separates expected and claimable value; keep those quantities distinct as more sources arrive. The actual Morpho screenshot is evidence of hierarchy, not evidence that its risk model transfers to withdrawal claims. [Morpho vaults](https://app.morpho.org/vaults), observed 2026-09-11.

## Quote quality needs a state and a clock

“Best valid offer received” is appropriately narrower than a claim about the whole market. However, the current screen supplies no visible last-checked time even though the data model carries an update timestamp. A buyer's funding can change after verification. Quote deadline, chain read freshness, discovery success, and transaction simulation answer different questions; one green status cannot stand for all four.

CoW's intent reference treats tokens, amounts, fees, receiver and validity as distinct settlement inputs. Uniswap's failure guidance separately explains deadline expiry, insufficient network funds and slippage protection. These support an interaction principle, not a recommendation to add AMM slippage: expose the constraint that stopped this exact action and a relevant next step. EXIT's sale is exact-or-revert, so a refresh must never silently authorize a changed price. [CoW intents](https://docs.cow.fi/cow-protocol/reference/core/intents), [Uniswap transaction failures](https://support.uniswap.org/hc/en-us/articles/8643975058829-Why-did-my-transaction-fail), accessed 2026-09-11; the Uniswap page was indexed as updated in 2026, and no exact revision was exposed in the retrieved text.

A firsthand CoW forum analysis posted on 2026-07-24 reports that apparent matching opportunities often disappeared after checking balances, allowances, cancellation and expiry. It also documents flaws and selection biases in its own analysis. This is a useful hypothesis generator for EXIT's “executable now” presentation, not evidence for a universal dead-order percentage or EXIT liquidity. A later participant response is likewise an unverified practitioner account. [CoW forum discussion](https://forum.cow.fi/t/ring-feasibility-zombie-orders-and-why-23-23-profitable-rings-turned-out-to-be-0-14/3511), accessed 2026-09-11.

Two bounded changes follow. First, show **chain state last checked** using the existing timestamp; explicitly avoid describing it as the age of every offer. Second, replace “No offers received yet” with “No active offers” when a successful discovery returns nothing. Native expiry means an empty query cannot establish that no offer ever existed. Failed discovery needs its own state: “Offers unavailable; retry discovery,” while onchain collection remains available. Do not hide a transport failure behind a friendly empty market.

## Reload is part of the financial workflow

A concrete reproduction selected claim #2, opened its trade view, selected Private Offers, and reloaded. The URL had remained `/`. Reload returned to Markets; reopening the seller screen selected claim #1 in Public offers. No funds were affected in this read-only session. Nevertheless, context and negotiation preference were lost at precisely the moment a returning user expects continuity.

Persist the page, claim identifier, and offer mode in a validated route, preserving existing preview parameters. Back and Forward should restore the same context. Keep prices, signatures, decryption keys and private payloads out of URLs. An unavailable claim should produce a missing-claim state, never quietly substitute the first claim. Restoring a route must not open a sale review, reconnect a wallet without its existing authorization, or submit anything. This is the first selected improvement because it reduces both ordinary navigation friction and accidental privacy-mode changes.

Transaction recovery is a separate, larger concern. Uniswap issue #6294, opened 2023-04-03, reports a UI remaining pending after onchain cancellation/replacement. It is a user bug report, not proof of current Uniswap behavior. Its lesson is to retain enough transaction identity to reconcile chain outcome rather than requiring the user to clear browser data. EXIT already has replacement handling and confirmed-result preservation; this session did not initiate transactions, so it did not verify pending-state persistence across reload. A future test must cover receipt recovery, cancellation, replacement with changed calldata, and an unavailable indexer after confirmation. [Uniswap issue #6294](https://github.com/Uniswap/interface/issues/6294), accessed 2026-09-11.

## Buyer accounting should explain ownership periods

The current Portfolio equation is now labeled “Net cost after withdrawals,” avoiding the earlier confusing negative “Unrecovered cost” wording. The model separately carries viewer withdrawals and globally removed claim value. This is the right boundary: buyer B must not inherit buyer A's 4,000 cash as personal income. The reference result remains A: 4,000 collected plus 5,985 resale minus 9,960 cost = 25; B: 6,000 collected minus 5,985 cost = 15. These are specified examples, not outcomes observed in this new session.

The fresh local wallet owned originated claims, so purchase cost displayed a dash. That is preferable to inventing an acquisition price, but the dash should eventually have an explanation such as “Originated position; no marketplace purchase.” Separate a source deposit from a secondary purchase if origination accounting is added. Closed positions should remain available to reconcile sale proceeds, withdrawn cash and realized result. A concise per-owner cash ledger is more valuable than a portfolio chart without an auditable basis. Keep later recovery rights visible when ordinary payout is exhausted.

## Accessibility findings with practical scope

At 390px and 320px, observed Markets and Claim detail had document widths equal to viewport widths. The wide market table scrolled within its own region. WCAG's Reflow explanation permits a two-dimensional exception for data tables, while ordinary surrounding content should reflow at an equivalent 320 CSS-pixel width. This observation supports narrow-layout usability, but does not establish browser zoom, text-spacing, assistive-technology, or whole-application conformance. [W3C Reflow guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html), accessed 2026-09-11.

The maker modal has a visible heading, but its Chromium accessibility snapshot begins with an unnamed dialog. The WAI modal pattern calls for an accessible dialog label and specifies expected focus behavior, including return to the invoking element when appropriate. In the current development build, Escape closed the modal but left focus on the body; the next Tab reached the device-key section below the application. The source effect captures active focus while calling `showModal`, without closing the dialog during its cleanup; React StrictMode repetition is a plausible cause, not yet a production reproduction. Add a stable heading association, use the actual dialog ref, and test focus return in both development and production rendering. [WAI modal dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/), accessed 2026-09-11.

A sampled Refresh text button was only 15 CSS pixels high. That alone does not prove a target-size violation: WCAG 2.5.8 includes spacing and equivalent-control exceptions. Increasing its hit area is nevertheless inexpensive and useful on touch devices. Several explanatory labels are 9–11px; that is a usability concern, not by itself a WCAG failure. Contrast and text resizing require targeted measurement before claiming a pass or fail. [W3C target-size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), updated 2026-05-11; [contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), accessed 2026-09-11.

Freshness messaging should avoid creating a screen-reader announcement every second. W3C status-message guidance supports announcing meaningful waiting, success and error transitions without moving focus, and warns against excessive live-region chatter. Announce discovery failure or recovery; present the changing last-checked value as ordinary text. [W3C status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html), accessed 2026-09-11.

## Recommended implementation order and evidence limits

1. Preserve page, claim and negotiation mode through reload and Back/Forward; handle invalid or missing claims explicitly. Test existing preview flags and ensure sensitive terms never enter the URL.
2. Distinguish no active offers from unavailable discovery. Show chain-check time accurately and retain collection actions during discovery failures.
3. Name trade dialogs and restore focus. Add a keyboard regression that opens, dismisses and resumes at the invoking action.
4. Consider sale-versus-wait comparison and owner-specific cash explanation after the economics workstream establishes meaningful assumptions. Defer annualized return decoration.

Comparable screens were browsed without connecting wallets. CoW initially showed a cross-chain promotional interstitial, so that capture alone does not establish quote-screen behavior; its primary documentation supplied the settlement comparison. Official X access returned HTTP 403, and targeted indexed searches did not yield a usable relevant firsthand post. No X claim was synthesized from inaccessible content. Articles and forums are supporting context; observed EXIT reproductions and canonical P1–P7 govern implementation. The next UI commit will be separate from this baseline research so improvements cannot be mistaken for pre-fix observations.

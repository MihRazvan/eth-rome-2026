# EXIT market landscape and bounded product choices

Research date: 11 September 2026. Scope: comparable products and economics; no new source admission, asset recommendation, transaction, or competitor audit. Repository evidence was read at pinned commits. Product descriptions, source behavior, deployment records, and demonstrated adoption are different evidence classes.

EXIT has direct prior art. Its defensible proposition is the combination of independently funded competing buyers, explicit residual ownership, collection and resale, and optional confidential negotiation with public settlement. A successful demonstration should make those properties observable. An attractive discount screen alone does not establish a market, reliable liquidity, or profitable underwriting.

| Comparable | Mechanism examined | Relevant lesson | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intentional | Atomic factoring of delayed claims | Exact acquisition and payment; operator funding discipline | Small mainnet pre-alpha claimed in repository; one factor per inspected kernel |
| TenderSwap / lpETH | Shared liquidity replenished by selling queued unlocks | Inventory duration, concentration and liquidity availability affect prices | Documentation and source disagree in consequential places |
| Unstake.it / Sanctum | Native Solana stake-account liquidity | Transfer withdrawal authority, replenish liquid inventory | Different chain and authority model; old brand is historical |
| Lido withdrawal queue | Transferable claim NFT and finalization | Current claimability and ownership outrank appearance or estimates | A source mechanism, not evidence of an active competitive claim market |
| Pendle / 0x | Signed orders and RFQ execution | Expiry, spendability and cancellation need distinct states | Fungible markets and established maker networks are not automatically available to EXIT |

## Intentional: close prior art, with a narrower market structure

Intentional's current README describes two Lido routes: selling stETH while originating a withdrawal, and selling an existing unstETH receipt. It labels the service a capped, unaudited Ethereum mainnet pre-alpha, with operator pricing and small trade limits. That is stronger evidence of an implemented comparable than a presentation, but does not prove sustained demand or independent market makers. [Repository overview](https://github.com/zkoranges/intentional/tree/63a3bd281fae21c99eba29bb3b4a3630d24b56d1)

The inspected kernel binds `factorSigner` immutably, requires the quoted factor to match it, authenticates the seller's direct call, checks quote expiry and nonce, and validates signatures at execution. This is a single-factor settlement deployment, not the same market structure as multiple independently funded EXIT makers. The useful comparison is authority and payment correctness; a list of many quotes does not itself establish multiple capital providers. [Pinned settlement code](https://github.com/zkoranges/intentional/blob/63a3bd281fae21c99eba29bb3b4a3630d24b56d1/src/claims/AsyncClaimSettlement.sol#L71)

Deployment provenance matters. The older manifest says `retired-paused`; a separate pre-alpha manifest says `active` and records an existing-NFT sale paying 0.0049875 WETH. Never use the retired addresses as current integrations. An attempted independent receipt read through Ethereum PublicNode failed with an HTTP error, and the explorer page could not be retrieved through this research browser. Consequently the transaction and active status remain developer-recorded evidence here, not independently verified September availability or adoption. [Retired manifest](https://github.com/zkoranges/intentional/blob/63a3bd281fae21c99eba29bb3b4a3630d24b56d1/deployments/mainnet-v2.json), [Pre-alpha manifest](https://github.com/zkoranges/intentional/blob/63a3bd281fae21c99eba29bb3b4a3630d24b56d1/deployments/mainnet-pre-alpha-001.json)

EXIT should publish per-maker authorization and realized collection evidence, including adverse outcomes. Competing quotes, an actual purchase, and residual resale are a more credible differentiation than claiming to invent factoring.

## TenderSwap and lpETH: liquidity is an inventory problem

TenderSwap's white paper describes a shared underlying-asset pool, utilization-sensitive fees, and inventory of withdrawal NFTs. Buyers replenish immediately available liquidity by purchasing unlocks; relayers later redeem mature inventory. This connects seller pricing to liquidity consumption and source concentration, rather than only elapsed time. Shared underlying denominations do not establish identical timing or loss risk. [White paper](https://docs.tenderize.me/tenderswap/white-paper)

There is a concrete description/code discrepancy. At commit `298fb55`, `buyUnlock` says the purchase reward decays toward maturity, but computes `baseReward * (1 - remainingTime / unlockTime)`. Holding the denominator and base reward constant, 75% time remaining yields 25% of the base reward; 25% remaining yields 75%. The code therefore increases the discount toward maturity within that interval, and rejects already mature unlocks. This is a source-level observation, not a claim about an identified deployed version or an exploit. It is sufficient reason not to copy the prose as a pricing specification. [Pinned buyUnlock](https://github.com/Tenderize/TenderSwap/blob/298fb55e170fa95856acf9bf0f67af471d33c7aa/src/Swap.sol#L348)

The successor lpETH path adds an expected token ID and checks actual finalization, a useful protection against buying different queue inventory than the buyer inspected. However, its pinned reward helper subtracts `block.timestamp` from unsigned `requestCreatedAt`; for an older request that expression underflows under the file's Solidity 0.8 checked arithmetic. The inspected source is not enough to map this behavior to any currently deployed pool. Treat it as an unresolved source/version discrepancy, not evidence that a live product is broken. [Pinned lpETH purchase and helper](https://github.com/Tenderize/lpETH/blob/0d2cf93e62a366963313deda6c894542fd607426/src/lpETH/LpETH.sol#L410)

The October 2024 launch article also advertises third-party incentives alongside swap fees. Incentive-funded LP returns are not proof that withdrawal discounts alone sustain the business. EXIT should measure capital committed, cash available, duration of acquired exposure, and collections separately; introducing pooled LP shares, emissions or governance incentives would add obligations outside the present product. [lpETH launch article](https://blog.tenderize.me/lpeth-pre-launch-a-new-era-for-staked-liquidity-copy/)

## Unstake.it: useful authority model, stale brand evidence

The accessible unstake.it site describes instant SOL liquidity in exchange for a native stake account, with the pool subsequently deactivating and withdrawing stake. Its economic mechanism transfers waiting to a liquidity provider. It does not establish that an Ethereum withdrawal NFT, or a BENQI caller-bound request, has interchangeable transfer semantics. [Unstake.it product explanation](https://www.unstake.it/)

Sanctum's official August 17, 2023 announcement states that unstake.it rebranded to Sanctum, broadened its liquidity offerings, and would wind down the old social account. A still-accessible old website is therefore not evidence of a separate current competitor or current audit posture. EXIT source dossiers should identify the actual withdrawal authority and servicing action, not classify every delayed asset as a transferable token. [Official rebrand](https://sanctum.so/blog/unstake-it-rebrands-to-sanctum-solana-lst-liquidity)

## Lido: claim status is more useful than an NFT picture

Lido's queue documentation makes the withdrawal NFT a right to claim finalized ETH; ownership is transferable and claiming burns the NFT. Requested stETH, finalizable payout, and rewards while waiting are distinct quantities. EXIT should preserve equally explicit units and states rather than displaying the original requested amount as guaranteed cash. Selling the liquid staking token before requesting withdrawal is also a different economic alternative from selling an already queued claim. [WithdrawalQueueERC721](https://docs.lido.fi/contracts/withdrawal-queue-erc721/), [Lido contract and withdrawal routes](https://docs.lido.fi/contracts/lido/)

A March 2024 firsthand forum complaint initially reads like evidence of an unexpectedly long queue. Reading the replies changes the conclusion: the original author later says the ETH was ready to claim while the NFT color had not changed. This supports a usability hypothesis—users may mistake presentation metadata for executable status—not a verified claim that the protocol delayed this withdrawal. The application should query authoritative claimability, show when it was checked, and distinguish “ready to collect” from “collected.” [Original discussion and author's correction](https://www.reddit.com/r/LidoFinance/comments/1br2nxz/)

Current dual-governance documentation adds a less obvious boundary: holders may escrow withdrawal NFTs for veto signaling. Certain Rage Quit flows impose further timelocks on proceeds. These are specific escrow circumstances, not the normal withdrawal queue duration. A generic NFT import must not assume that an escrow participant owns a freely transferable claim with ordinary timing. For EXIT this is a source-admission checklist improvement only; it does not enable Lido or governance-escrow assets. [Dual governance guide](https://docs.lido.fi/guides/dg-guide/)

## Pendle and RFQ: borrow order discipline, not asset equivalence

Pendle combines a PT/YT order book with AMM routing and explicitly distinguishes active, executed, expired, cancelled and invalid orders. Balance and allowance changes affect fillability. These are useful operational patterns even though a fungible principal token with an expiry is not a bespoke residual claim with source-specific recovery. Small orders also face execution-cost tradeoffs; counting posted quotes without measuring actual fills can conceal poor liquidity. [Order book mechanics](https://docs.pendle.finance/pendle-v2/ProtocolMechanics/LiquidityEngines/OrderBook)

Its documented order struct includes expiry, maker nonce, beneficiary and a conversion-rate guard; cancellation and onchain presigning are separate operations. EXIT already has its own exact signed schema and should keep it. The transferable lesson is that an offchain order remains contingent on current executable state. The implementation link in these docs points to `pendle-core-v2`, but this pass could not retrieve that linked code; its documented behavior is not recorded as independently inspected source. [Limit order contract documentation](https://docs.pendle.finance/pendle-v2-dev/LimitOrder/LimitOrderContract)

0x describes private offchain maker quotes with public settlement and roughly minute-long mainnet validity, recommending frequent refresh. Its RFQ network is a custom-plan service, so it is not a ready-made buyer pool for withdrawal claims. Neither its promotional pricing statistics nor its freshness interval should be transplanted as EXIT evidence. Refresh state checks frequently, but preserve the user's selected signed terms and require explicit acceptance when a replacement changes economics. [0x RFQ explanation](https://help.0x.org/articles/3310442497-understanding-0x-rfq-request-for-quote)

## Bounded choices for EXIT

These are proposed changes, not assertions that they are already implemented:

1. **Make buyer accounting decision-ready.** Show acquisition cost including known fees, cash actually collected, cash withdrawn, and remaining rights. A 1,000-unit requested claim bought for 970 has a nominal 30-unit spread; if total realized proceeds are 940, the loss is 30 before gas. After collecting 400, break-even requires another 570 before additional costs. This arithmetic is illustrative, not a payout forecast.
2. **Use timing scenarios rather than a headline promised APY.** Let the same price be inspected under earlier, expected and delayed collection, with loss shown independently. A source estimate must carry its provenance and observation time. Once partial proceeds arrive, recompute remaining exposure without rewriting historic cost or declaring final exhaustion from zero routine views.
3. **Explain quote invalidity precisely.** Separate deadline expiry, maker cancellation, ownership change, depletion change, insufficient maker balance, missing allowance and failed observation. An unavailable RPC is “not checked,” not “invalid.” Record simulation time/block, and retain the warning that multiple quotes do not reserve maker cash. This can be implemented without changing the contract or enabling partial order fills.
4. **Measure the market, not the quote counter.** Report independently controlled funded makers, executable quotes per request, time to first executable offer, seller net after explicit fees, settlement failure reason, and realized buyer collections. Keep local/testnet measurements labeled. Private-mode statistics must be computed only from permitted visibility or post-settlement public data; never export competing plaintext bids as analytics.
5. **Make alternatives contextual.** For already claimable positions, collecting directly is an immediate comparison; for a queued nontransferable EOA request, clearly explain that import is unsupported. Do not suggest that an LST swap is available after that LST has been burned or locked. Add precise source/status language without expanding enabled sources or adding an external swap router.

## Evidence limits and next validation

This pass inspected three open repositories at immutable commits, primary product documentation, publisher articles and a complete firsthand forum thread. It did not measure active market depth, independently corroborate mainnet transaction receipts, or reproduce competitor contracts. X profile retrieval returned no post content and targeted searches found no inspectable relevant thread; no social engagement or attributed X claim is inferred. The source inventory preserves these distinctions and publication dates where available.

The next useful evidence is EXIT's own reproducible funded-maker, collection, loss and resale ledger. Competitor references establish mechanisms and pitfalls; they do not replace source admission, live sponsor round-trips, or a runnable public demonstration.
